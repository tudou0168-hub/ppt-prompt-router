# Universal Professional Visual Prior — Experimental Style Specification

> Method and design defaults only. No project communication contract, brand identity, page structure, or SVG prototypes.

## I. Style Overview

| Property | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Style Name | Universal Professional Visual Prior (Experiment) |
| Best Fit | Any presentation whose content, evidence and audience differ but whose pages must make a clear professional judgment |
| Reusable Intent | Convert page task, core message, content relationship, hierarchy and evidence role into a deliberate visual reading order without fixing brand, geometry or page type |
| Sources | PPT Master v2.8 / v2.9 visual-DNA archaeology, adapted only through current v4.5 native interfaces |

## II. Communication Method

- **Argument Flow**: Begin with the audience change required on each page. Establish one core message, identify the material-supported relationship and evidence, then choose a reading order that makes the message and relationship immediately legible.
- **Claim Discipline**: Keep fact, inference, assumption, implication and recommendation distinct. Treat a conclusion as a claim that must retain its material-supported basis and make unresolved evidence visible.
- **Page Message Discipline**: One page has one dominant conclusion or communication action. Supporting content proves, explains, compares, qualifies or operationalizes it; topic-only titles and equal-weight content inventories are insufficient.
- **Relationship Discipline**: Use visual structure when the content has a real causal, temporal, hierarchical, comparative, dependency, system or evidence relationship. When it does not, retain direct readable composition rather than inventing a diagram.
- **Hierarchy Discipline**: Distinguish core message, primary evidence or relation, secondary support and annotation through visual weight and whitespace. Do not use identical containers as the default expression of different importance.

## III. Page Role Vocabulary

| Role | Communication Job | Evidence Obligation | Composition Tendency |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Recognition | Establish a shared understanding | Show the minimum context that makes the idea credible | Let the governing idea lead, with context receding |
| Diagnosis | Reveal a condition, gap or cause | Separate fact, inference and unresolved baseline | Make the meaningful contrast or dependency visible |
| Structure | Explain a system, mechanism or hierarchy | Preserve interfaces, layers and ownership supported by the material | Let the governing relationship occupy the main space |
| Evidence | Prove an outcome or comparison | Keep units, source, period and limitations near the claim | Let the decision-relevant evidence dominate over furniture |
| Decision | Enable a choice or action | Show rationale, trade-offs, dependencies and remaining uncertainty | Keep the action and its basis visibly connected |
| Progression | Explain a process, route or learning sequence | Preserve order, gates, feedback and outcomes | Let sequence and conditional movement be scannable |

## IV. Evidence & Data Expression

- Select charts, tables, screenshots, photos and diagrams from what they prove or explain. Do not use them as decoration.
- **Argument Trace**: Keep each page message connected to the material-supported evidence, comparison, mechanism, decision condition or stated uncertainty that makes it credible.
- For quantitative claims, retain the comparison logic and source context present in the material; do not invent targets, benchmarks or trends.
- **Charts**: Use a chart when magnitude, trend, composition, comparison or distribution is the evidence; preserve the relevant unit, period and labels.
- **Tables**: Use a table when exact values, conditions, accountability or a cross-reference is the real task; retain a clear scan path rather than forcing a diagram.
- **Sources**: Keep source, period, scope and known limitations close to the claim whenever the material supplies them; mark absence rather than inventing provenance.
- **Native Editability**: Prefer editable native charts, tables and business shapes when they carry a changing or reviewable claim. Preserve high-fidelity screenshots and source evidence as images when redrawing would weaken the fact.
- For tables and evidence-dense pages, favor accuracy, legibility and one clear scan path. A grid is valid when the information is genuinely grid-like.

## V. Visual System Defaults

- **Composition**: Start from one page-scale spatial action. Prefer one to three primary regions unless the content genuinely requires more. Make the first visual focus align with the core message.
- **Density**: Let synthesis, decision and transition pages breathe; allow evidence, table and detail pages to become denser while preserving hierarchy and readable scale.
- **Decoration**: Use visual elements only to encode hierarchy, grouping, direction, evidence or atmosphere that serves the message. Avoid ornamental containers, badges and effects that compete with content.
- **Color Behavior**: Let the current project identity decide colors. Reserve contrast for semantic focus, difference, change, risk, evidence or action; do not introduce a universal palette.
- **Typography Character**: Let current project typography decide families. Use scale, weight, alignment and spacing to establish hierarchy before adding containers.

## VI. Image & Icon Direction

- **Image Usage**: Use imagery when it adds proof, context, causal explanation or a genuine focal subject. Keep captions, source and claim connected when relevant.
- **Image Treatment**: Crop and integrate images around their evidentiary subject; use framing, overlays or whitespace only where they improve reading.
- **Icon Treatment**: Icons may clarify role, state or relationship. They are not a substitute for hierarchy or a mandatory repeated motif.

## VII. Review Focus

> Apply this section only after the user explicitly activates visual review. It never triggers that stage.

- At rendered size, the first visual focus expresses the page's core message rather than decoration or an equal-weight module set.
- A real content relationship is spatially legible when the page claims one; no diagram exists merely to avoid cards.
- Evidence, support and annotation are visibly subordinate or dominant according to their stated role.
- Tables, data and factual modules remain clear when they are the most accurate expression.
- Adjacent pages share a visual language while their page-scale actions and density respond to the story.
